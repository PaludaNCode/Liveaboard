How the numbers are built Generated __GENERATED__.

True cost

The advertised berth price, plus every fee a diver cannot avoid: marine park charges, port and harbour dues, fuel surcharges and the visa on arrival. Optional extras are added only when you switch them on above.

What lands later

The gap between the advertised price and what you actually pay. Sort by it to see which trips hold back the most. There is no rating of operators here: two boats can reach the same true cost by pricing very differently, and the breakdown shows how.

Unpriced

Extras the operator lists without a number — “Rental Gear” named and left blank. Not zero and not absent: you will be billed, and the listing does not say how much. The count says how incomplete a true cost figure is.

Nitrox and tips

Nitrox says included, a price, or extra, no price — roughly half this fleet bundles it and half bills for it. Crew tips are counted wherever an operator publishes a figure; where one is expected but no amount is given the total reads + tips, because that cost is real and sits outside the arithmetic.

Per dive

Marked ~ because the denominator is worked out, not published: three dives for every full day at sea, so eighteen on a seven-night week — the figure the two vessels that state one both give. Erring low on purpose. Assuming more dives would divide the bill by a bigger number and make every trip look cheaper than it is.

Places

Straight from the operator’s own listing: available, a few left, or sold out. A hundred and twenty-seven of these departures are already full, and pricing them beside bookable ones without saying so would put trips nobody can buy at the top of a cheapest-first sort.

Disclosure

Whether the operator published its required extras at all. A listing naming only optional ones gets no true cost: every Egyptian liveaboard pays park and port fees, so silence means either bundled or collected at the dock, and it does not say which.

Dive sites

Read from the operator’s own trip name, which is where they name what the week actually visits. Shown instead of a route label: the label was our summary of the sites, and summarising a list you can simply read loses more than it saves.

Currency

Everything is shown in euro. Amounts an operator quoted in another currency are marked as converted, with the rate and its date, because a conversion is a weaker claim than a quote.
